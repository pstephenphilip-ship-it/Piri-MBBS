## Practical
Search extracts say the specification suggests "investigating the effectiveness of corrosion prevention (barrier and sacrificial protection methods)". I could not confirm a J260 PAG code, so the slides use the label **SUGGESTED PRACTICAL**.
- **Slide 5:** the classic three-tube conditions experiment (air + water; boiled water + oil; dry air with calcium chloride).
- **Slide 11** covers method, apparatus and safety. The apparatus drawing shows 5 tubes (tap water, salt water, painted, zinc-wrapped, copper-wrapped) and a 0.001 g balance. IV is the protection method, DV is mass gain in mg, and the controls are listed. Safety follows CLEAPSS: eye protection, salt solution low hazard, and anhydrous calcium chloride IRRITANT (in the notes).
- **Slide 12** covers results and errors. It has a results table and a graph to scale of mass gain against days for A and B. It works out rates of 6.4 and 13 mg/day, a ratio of 2.0, and the effect of copper. Errors listed: rust flaking off, wet nails, variation between nails. Improvements: repeats and means.

## Maths (52 asserted calcs; all data illustrative unless stated)
- Nail mass gain: 0.06 / 5.00 = 1.2%.
- Paint for railings: 36 m², so 3 tins.
- Zinc blocks: 96 kg used over 4 years is 24 kg per year.
- Practical:
  - A gains 0.90%.
  - Rates are 45 ÷ 7 = 6.4 and 90 ÷ 7 = 13 mg/day.
  - The B : A ratio is 2.0.
  - QC: 39 mg gained between day 2 and day 5.
- Glass bottle LCA: total 5.0 MJ, of which manufacture is 52%.
- Bags:
  - totals 0.80 and 1.80 MJ, a ratio of 2.25
  - per use 0.16 and 1.80 MJ, about 11 times
  - QC cotton bag: 0.12 MJ per use
- Waste: 180 kg recycled; 120 kg (30%) to landfill.
- PET: 25 bottles per fleece.
- Aluminium: the 95% energy saving is a widely quoted figure. 170 MJ/kg from ore is illustrative. 7.5 kg of cans saves about 1200 MJ.
- Steel: 60% saving. Copper: 85% saving.
- FQ: painting needs 1140 GJ and galvanising 1050 GJ.
