## Practical: PAG C4 Production of salts
- **Salt used.** C5.1 already did copper sulfate crystals (CuO + H₂SO₄). This deck makes **magnesium sulfate (Epsom salts)** from **MgCO₃ + 0.50 mol/dm³ H₂SO₄**. Both are low hazard, and the end point is visible because the fizzing stops.
- **Slide 19 (method):**
  - Four stages drawn: beaker with excess carbonate, filter funnel and flask, evaporating basin on a water bath, crystals.
  - Method, variables and CLEAPSS-style safety.
  - Worked example: 2.50 − 1.43 = 1.07 g reacted.
- **Slide 20 (results):**
  - pH-meter readings while MgCO₃ is added, with a results table and a graph to scale (pH against mass).
  - Before the end point the curve is computed in node; the readings after it are illustrative.
  - End point 1.05 g. HT worked example: 0.0125 mol × 84.3 = 1.05 g.
  - Conclusion, errors and improvements.
- **Hazards (CLEAPSS style):**
  - H₂SO₄ 0.5–1.5 mol/dm³: IRRITANT
  - HCl below 2 mol/dm³: LOW HAZARD
  - NaOH 0.05–0.5 mol/dm³: IRRITANT (CORROSIVE from 0.5 mol/dm³)
  - Mg ribbon: FLAMMABLE
  - H₂: EXTREMELY FLAMMABLE
  - limewater: eye IRRITANT
  - UI solution: HIGHLY FLAMMABLE (ethanol)
  - CuO: HARMFUL / toxic to aquatic life
  - MgCO₃, MgSO₄: low hazard
- **No percentage yield.** That is C6.3.

## Maths (59 asserted calcs; Ar to 1 d.p.)
- **Mr:** MgCO₃ 84.3, H₂SO₄ 98.1, MgSO₄ 120.4, HCl 36.5, CH₃COOH 60.0.
- **pH ladder 1–7:** each step is a factor of 10.
- **Dilution:**
  - ×10 raises the pH by 1
  - pH 2 diluted ×100 gives pH 4
  - pH 2 vs pH 5 is a factor of 1000
- **Strong vs weak** (Ka of ethanoic acid = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵):
  - 0.10 mol/dm³ ethanoic acid: pH 2.9, 1.3% ionised
  - HCl at the same concentration: pH 1.0, about 76× more H⁺
- **Concentration:** 7.3 / 73 g/dm³ = 0.20 / 2.0 mol/dm³; QC 0.20 mol/dm³ ethanoic acid.
- **PAG:**
  - 0.0125 mol H₂SO₄ needs 1.05 g MgCO₃
  - modelled pH readings 0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 1.3
  - post-end-point line 6.9 and 7.6
